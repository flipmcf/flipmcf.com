Two URLs. That's the Whole API.

A video player asks for a lot of files under the hood - media playlists, segments, and so on. You never type those by hand. As a publisher, there are really only two endpoints you need to know.

The .csmil Endpoint — Point Your Site Here

The flagship. Feed it a comma-separated list of your already-encoded renditions and it packages them into an HLS stream on the fly, caches the result, and serves it. Packaging is CPU-cheap, so your server does almost nothing per request while your storage does the work.

/i/my_video,360p,480p,720p,.mp4.csmil/master.m3u8

It uses the same URL grammar Akamai Media Services On Demand used, so if you're migrating off Akamai, your existing player integrations mostly just work — no rewrite.

The /i/abr/ Endpoint — Encodes For You

Haven't encoded Adaptive Bitrate renditions yet? Point here instead. It's a state machine, not a file server:

/i/abr/my_video.mp4/master.m3u8

Renditions already on disk? It 302-redirects straight to the .csmil URL and gets out of the way. Nothing exists yet? It queues your full ABR ladder in the background and instantly hands your viewer a fast, single-rendition stream so they're watching in seconds, not staring at a spinner. Once the real renditions land, later requests redirect to .csmil automatically.
